# Tether customer-obsession doctrine

Tether earns attention by making music more relational. Every product decision
must improve one of four customer outcomes: hear something together, understand
someone through music, express what music meant, or preserve a shared memory.

## Product promises

1. **Value before explanation.** The first screen presents a meaningful action,
   not product taxonomy, implementation detail, or internal evidence language.
2. **One obvious next step.** Every state offers a primary action that advances
   the customer’s current goal. Secondary actions remain available without
   competing with it.
3. **Agency is not a premium feature.** Filters, privacy, safety, complete
   profiles, blocking, reporting, and reversibility are core product quality.
4. **Trust is visible and calm.** Claims identify whether they are observed,
   self-declared, local to the demo, or designed previews. Truth never becomes
   clutter or institutional language the customer must decode.
5. **Relationships outrank reach.** Ranking, notifications, and growth mechanics
   should deepen relevant relationships rather than maximize raw engagement.
6. **Music provides the opening.** Discovery and Dating begin with songs,
   listening behavior, communities, or shared context—not empty likes or
   unrestricted cold messages.
7. **Privacy behaves exactly as described.** Every visible setting changes
   eligibility, disclosure, ranking, or retention. Decorative privacy controls
   are prohibited.
8. **Recovery is designed.** Passes can be undone, visibility can be paused,
   private access can be revoked, and failures preserve the customer’s work.
9. **Accessibility is a completion criterion.** Readable type, 44-pixel targets,
   semantic navigation, keyboard operation, reduced motion, and clear focus
   states ship with the feature.
10. **Delight follows meaning.** Motion, color, celebration, and sound reward a
    real human moment. They never disguise waiting, uncertainty, or missing
    functionality.

## Review questions

Before merging a customer-facing change, answer:

- What does the customer accomplish within ten seconds?
- Is the primary action unmistakable?
- Does every displayed control have a real effect?
- Can the customer understand why something was shown?
- Can they reverse the important decision?
- Are privacy and safety available at the moment they matter?
- Does the screen remain readable and operable on a 320-pixel-wide device?
- Does an empty, loading, error, or interrupted state preserve dignity and
  provide a useful next action?
- Does the experience lead naturally toward a shared listen, expression,
  relationship, or memory?
