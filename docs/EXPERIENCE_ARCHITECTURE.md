# Tether Experience Architecture

## Product hierarchy

Tether is not five equally important utilities. It is one relationship loop:

1. **Hear** — notice that someone is listening.
2. **Enter** — join the exact musical moment.
3. **Express** — use Pulse, conversation, or cultural writing.
4. **Remember** — preserve the person, song, and feeling.
5. **Return** — use that history to find the next meaningful moment.

The live Tether is the emotional climax. Every other surface must either create a reason to Tether, deepen the relationship inside it, or make the result worth returning to.

## Placement doctrine

| Customer intention | Primary home | What belongs there | What does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| Listen with someone | Listen | Current playback, people listening now, requests, relationship continuation | Long feeds, local charts, account tools |
| Participate in music culture | Exchange | Reviews, discussions, diary, lists, local listening activity | Dating controls, private relationship history |
| Find or talk to people | People | Messages, live friends, communities, local scenes, search | Public criticism, account configuration |
| Date through music | People → Dating | Discover, Browse, Signals, matches, Dating profile and safety | Generic friend discovery, public DMs |
| Manage identity and memory | You | Profiles, music library, shared identities, Anchors, Capsules, privacy, account data | Live discovery feeds |
| Enter a shared moment | Tether | Track, people, sync, playback controls, Pulse | Navigation, recommendations, promotional content |

## Screen rules

### Listen

- Current playback owns the first viewport.
- “Listening now” is the next strongest object.
- Requests are urgent but visually quieter than playback.
- Recommendations and memory are progressive disclosure under “For later.”
- Imported-library confirmation belongs under identity, not on every return to Listen.

### Exchange

- Show cultural content before tool explanation.
- One composer opens every creation workflow.
- Exchange, Diary, and Lists are the only top-level modes.
- Feed filters are secondary controls.
- Review actions must use ordinary language.

### People

- “People” is the navigation label; Wavelength remains the underlying discovery idea.
- People and Dating are mutually clear modes, not nested marketing cards.
- Messages begin collapsed so availability and connection are immediately visible.
- Friends, communities, local scenes, and search are simple secondary sections.

### Dating

- Dating becomes visually self-contained when selected.
- The candidate appears in the first useful viewport.
- Profile configuration lives under Profile, not above every candidate.
- Discover is editorial and contextual.
- Browse is dense and fast.
- Signals are musical openings, never unrestricted cold messages.
- Filters, privacy, Undo, blocking, and reporting remain free.

### You

- Identity is the hero.
- Tools are grouped by customer intention: Profiles, Music library, Relationships, Account.
- Memory objects remain emotionally distinct from account administration.
- Connected-provider receipts live here.

### Tether and Pulse

- A session removes the rest of the product from attention.
- Album art, the other person, and synchronization are the only persistent context.
- Pulse is a deliberate hold-and-release ritual.
- Charging should feel like two signals converging.
- Release should affect the entire stage, the listener avatars, and the artwork.
- The result is relational language: “Zuri felt your Pulse,” not a generic success toast.

## Visual system

- **Canvas:** near-black, warm rather than blue.
- **Text:** warm paper, never pure clinical white.
- **Pulse / urgency:** electric coral.
- **Presence / identity:** ultraviolet.
- **Synchronization:** cyan.
- **Memory / saved meaning:** warm yellow.
- **Cards:** used only for objects with a boundary; sections do not each need a box.
- **Typography:** large Space Grotesk display type, readable Outfit body type.
- **Motion:** ambient motion stays quiet; human arrival and Pulse are theatrical.

## Customer test

Every feature must pass five questions before it earns space:

1. What customer intention brought this person here?
2. Is this the most important next action?
3. Does the feature need to be visible now, or merely reachable?
4. Can the customer understand the action without branded vocabulary?
5. Does the result create connection, expression, memory, or control?
